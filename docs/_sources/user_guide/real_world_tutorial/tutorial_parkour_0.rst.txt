=============================
Simple Maze – Cartesian Space
=============================

.. admonition:: What you will learn
   :class: tip

   How to use several of pitasc's most common skills, including **linear
   movements**, a **guarded approach** and different ways of using the
   **slide** skill.

.. rst-class:: tutorial-meta

:bdg-primary:`Level: beginner` :bdg-secondary:`Cartesian space`
:bdg-secondary:`force control` :bdg-info:`~60 min setup`

Requirements
============

Hardware
--------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Item
     - Details
   * - 3D printer
     - For the parts listed in :ref:`parkour0-models`
   * - Fasteners
     - Screws ISO 4762 – M3x35 and nuts ISO 4032 – M3 (or comparable),
       **7 pcs each**
   * - Robot
     - Any pitasc-compatible robot with a force/torque sensor.
       This example uses a **UR5**.
   * - Table *(recommended)*
     - Welding table with 50 × 50 hole pattern, hole diameter 16 mm
   * - Positioning bolts
     - 16 mm, **4 – 6 pcs**

Setup
=====

Step 1 - Print the parts
------------------------

Print the lower and upper section, the cylindrical peg carrier and the cylindrical
peg. Also as per your gripper, print the custom gripper fingers to hold the cylindrical peg.

.. warning::
   Tolerances were tested on a **Bambu Lab X1** - adjust if needed..

Step 2 - Assemble the station
-----------------------------

Assemble the lower and upper section using the screws and nuts.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_0/simple_maze_assembly.png
   :alt: Assembling the parts
   :width: 90%
   :align: center

   Assembly of the 3D-printed parts with bottom stand and top layer with maze.

Step 3 - Clamp everything to the table
--------------------------------------

Clamp the assembled station and the workpiece carrier to your table.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_0/p0_example_configuration.jpeg
   :alt: Example table configuration
   :width: 90%
   :align: center

   Example configuration, allowing easy access to the cylindrical peg and a minimal
   distance between the cylindrical peg holder and the simple maze.

Step 4 - Position the cylindrical peg on the peg holder
-------------------------------------------------------

Place the cylindrical peg in the peg holder as shown below.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_0/peg_in_peg_holder.png
   :alt: Peg stand with cylindrical peg
   :width: 90%
   :align: center

   Cylindrical peg in the peg holder.

Step 5 - Mount the gripper fingers
----------------------------------

If needed, screw the custom 3D-printed gripper fingers onto the Weiss
WSG 50-110 gripper.


How to solve this parkour task
==============================

The task is to solve a maze using a cylindrical peg, as shown in the video
below.

.. video:: /_videos/real_world_tutorial/parkour_0/pitasc_realworld_tutorial_0.mp4
   :width: 100%

   Maze solving using a cylindrical peg

Try it yourself
---------------

You can try to solve the task on your own by selecting appropriate skills and
teaching the required poses.

.. card:: Where to put your solution

   Add your solution to:

   .. code-block:: text

      .../pitasc_realworld_tutorial_p0/apps/parkour_0.xml

   Then start RViz and the frame editor with:

   .. code-block:: bash

      roslaunch pitasc_realworld_tutorial_p0 bringup_ur5_parkour_0.launch

.. _list-rough-steps:

Level 1 – Rough steps
---------------------

The sample solution comprises the following rough steps. Each step is
implemented as a skill sequence containing several skills:

#. Pick up the rod from the holder.
#. Solve the maze by moving from the start position to the end position.
#. Place the rod back into the holder.


.. _example-steps:

Level 2 – Example steps in detail
---------------------------------

.. note::
   For safety reasons, each step begins and ends with the robot moving to its
   home pose by linear movement.

Step 1 - Pick up the rod from the holder
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*
#. Move to a position above the workpiece carrier *(position controlled)*
#. Lower the end effector to the gripping position *(position controlled)*
#. Grip the rod *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Push down on the rod to ensure a safe grip
   *(force controlled - only needed for flat gripper fingers)*
#. Move upwards *(position controlled)*
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*

Step 2 - Solve the maze
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose with the rod between the gripper fingers
   *(position controlled)*
#. Move linearly to a hover position above the maze *(position controlled)*
#. Move to the approach position close to the maze start point
   *(position controlled)*
#. Establish contact between rod and maze *(force controlled)*
#. Slide the rod through the maze while keeping a constant force of at least
   **5 N** between rod and maze *(force controlled)*.

   .. warning::
      Write this skill **without hardcoding** the maze waypoints.

   .. note::
      The example code moves the robot so that the rod reaches the goal
      position by utilising rod–wall interactions. The walls along the slot
      can be used to guide the rod using force feedback.

#. Once the rod reaches the goal position, the skill ends and the end effector
   moves upwards.
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*

Step 3 - Place the rod back into the holder
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*
#. Move to a position above the workpiece carrier *(position controlled)*
#. Place the rod in the holder by pushing down with a force of e.g. **10 N**
   *(force controlled)*
#. Open the gripper to release the rod *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Move back up to the pre-grasp pose *(position controlled)*
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*


.. _tips-on-skills:

Level 3 – Tips on using skills
------------------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 35 65

   * - Goal
     - Recommended approach
   * - Establish contact with the surroundings
     - Use a **guarded approach**.
   * - Predictable linear motion with minimal jerk
     - Use ``lin_time_optimal_ruckig``.
   * - Follow a wall under constant force
     - Use the pitasc **slide** skill.
   * - End a skill on a condition
     - Attach **monitors** to react to a force threshold or travelled distance.

.. danger::
   Always **tare the force/torque sensor** before running force-controlled
   skills.

.. tip::
   Add **user confirms** between skills. This makes it much easier to spot
   problems and abort execution safely while developing.
