.. _complex-maze-with-hatch:

=========================================
Complex Maze with Hatch - Cartesian Space
=========================================

.. admonition:: What you will learn
   :class: tip

   How to combine several of pitasc's most common skills - **linear
   movements**, a **guarded approach** and different variants of the **slide**
   skill. The application focus is twofold: **door opening**, which uses force
   feedback to find the rotation axis of the hatch, and **force-guided maze
   solving**, in which the peg is guided purely by contact forces.

.. rst-class:: tutorial-meta

:bdg-primary:`Level: advanced` :bdg-secondary:`door opening`
:bdg-secondary:`force control` :bdg-info:`slope & curve following`

Requirements
============

Hardware
--------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Item
     - Details
   * - 3D printer
     - For the parts listed in :ref:`p2-models`
   * - Fasteners
     - Screws ISO 4762 – M3x35 and nuts ISO 4032 – M3 (or comparable),
       **7 pcs each**
   * - Robot
     - Any pitasc-compatible robot with a force/torque sensor.
       This example uses a **UR5e** with a **Weiss WSG 50-110**.
   * - Table *(recommended)*
     - Welding table with 50 × 50 hole pattern, hole diameter 16 mm
   * - Positioning bolts
     - 16 mm, **4 – 6 pcs**

Setup
=====

Step 1 - Print the parts
------------------------

Print the lower and upper section, the cylindrical peg carrier and the cylindrical
peg. Also as per your gripper, print the custom gripper fingers to hold the cylindrical peg.

.. warning::
   Tolerances were tested on a **Bambu Lab X1** - adjust if needed..

Step 2 - Assemble the station
-----------------------------

Assemble the lower and upper section using the screws and nuts.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_0/09_complex_maze_assembly.png
   :alt: Assembling the parts
   :width: 90%
   :align: center

   Assembly of the 3D-printed parts with bottom stand and top layer with complex maze.


Step 3 - Attach the handle
--------------------------

Attach the desired handle (flat or spherical) to the side of the hatch using an
M3x25 screw and an M3 nut.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_2/05_attach_handle.png
   :alt: Attaching the handle to the hatch
   :width: 66%
   :align: center

   Handle mounted to the side of the hatch.

Step 4 - Mount the hatch
------------------------

Attach the hatch to the base maze part using two M3x25 screws and two M3 nuts.

.. warning::
   Tighten the nuts only **slightly**, so that the hatch can still rotate
   freely. An over-tightened hatch distorts the force feedback used to estimate
   the hinge axis.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_2/10_attach_the_door.png
   :alt: Attaching the hatch to the maze
   :width: 66%
   :align: center

   Hatch mounted to the upper maze section.

Step 5 - Clamp everything to the table
--------------------------------------

Place the maze and the peg holder on your welding table and fix them with the
positioning bolts.

.. figure:: /_images/real_world_tutorial/parkour_2/p2_example_configuration.png
   :alt: Complete setup on the welding table
   :width: 90%
   :align: center

   Example configuration, allowing easy access to both the peg holder and the
   maze entrance.

Step 6 - Mount the gripper fingers
----------------------------------

If needed, screw the custom 3D-printed gripper fingers onto the Weiss
WSG 50-110 gripper.


How to solve this parkour task
==============================

The task consists of several sub-tasks:

#. Open the hatch while continuously gripping the installed handle.
#. Pick up the cylindrical peg.
#. Push the peg down at the start of the maze.
#. Guide the peg along the upper path, keeping contact with the maze surface at
   all times.
#. Glide down the slope until the downward curve is reached.
#. Follow the curve and guide the peg into the target opening.
#. Place the peg back in the peg holder.

.. admonition:: Why the hatch matters
   :class: note

   The hinge position and axis of rotation are **not taught in advance**. They
   are estimated online from force feedback while the gripper holds the handle,
   which makes the skill robust against small placement errors of the station.

.. _p2-rough-steps:

Level 1 – Rough steps
---------------------

The sample solution comprises the following rough steps. Each step is
implemented as a skill sequence containing several skills:

#. Pick up the peg from the peg holder.
#. Place the peg back in the peg holder.
#. Open the hatch that blocks the maze.
#. Solve the maze by guiding the peg along the path while keeping it in contact
   with the maze surface.


.. _p2-example-steps:

Level 2 – Example steps in detail
---------------------------------

.. note::
   For safety reasons, each step begins and ends with the robot moving to its
   home pose by linear movement.

Step 1 - Pick up the peg
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*
#. Move to a position above the peg holder *(position controlled)*
#. Lower the end effector to the gripping position *(position controlled)*
#. Grip the cylindrical peg *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Push down on the peg to ensure a safe grip
   *(force controlled - only needed for flat gripper fingers)*
#. Move upwards *(position controlled)*
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*

Step 2 - Place down the peg
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*
#. Move to a position above the peg holder *(position controlled)*
#. Move close to the cylindrical opening *(position controlled)*
#. Establish contact between peg and opening *(force controlled)*
#. Push down to ensure the peg is seated inside the opening
   *(force controlled)*
#. Release the grip *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Move upwards *(position controlled)*
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*

Step 3 - Open the hatch
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*
#. Ensure the gripper is open *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Move the gripper to a horizontal pose above the handle
   *(position controlled)*
#. Move the gripper to the grasp pose for the handle *(position controlled)*
#. Grip the handle *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Rotate around the hatch hinge axis while still gripping the handle
   *(force and position controlled)*
#. Release the handle *(command to your specific gripper)*
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*

Step 4 - Solve the maze
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*
#. Move the peg above the maze start position *(position controlled)*
#. Establish contact between peg and maze *(force controlled)*
#. Slide while pushing down until a high force signals that a wall has been
   hit; change direction and slide again. Repeat until a certain depth is
   reached *(velocity, position and force controlled)*
#. On entering the slope, press down to determine the surface normal
   *(force controlled)*
#. Orient the peg along the direction of the normal force while pressing down
   *(position and force controlled)*
#. Slide down the slope while pushing down, until a high force signals the end
   of the slope *(velocity, position and force controlled)*
#. Rotate the peg upright, then push against the outside/inside of the curve
   while moving along the curve *(velocity, position and force controlled)*
#. Push the peg into the final target opening *(force controlled)*
#. Move upwards *(position controlled)*
#. Move to home pose *(position controlled)*

.. tip::
   The slope is the critical section: re-estimating the surface normal before
   sliding gives you a **well-defined contact state**, so the sliding direction
   and the pressing direction stay decoupled.


.. _p2-tips:

Level 3 – Tips on using skills
------------------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 35 65

   * - Goal
     - Recommended approach
   * - Press down *and* move at the same time
     - Combine both motions in a **skill concurrency**.
   * - Establish contact with the surroundings
     - Use a **guarded approach**.
   * - Predictable linear motion with minimal jerk
     - Use ``lin_time_optimal_ruckig``.
   * - Follow a wall, slope or curve under constant force
     - Use the pitasc **slide** skill.
   * - End a skill on a condition
     - Attach **monitors** to react to a force threshold or travelled distance.
   * - Align the tool with an unknown surface
     - Press down, read the measured force direction and orient the peg along
       the resulting normal.

.. danger::
   **Tare the force/torque sensor before every force-controlled skill.**
   This is the single most important step for reliable real-world operation -
   an untared sensor makes the measured contact force meaningless and can
   damage the peg, the hatch or the station.

.. tip::
   Add **user confirms** between skills. This makes it much easier to spot
   problems and abort execution safely while developing.
